Introduction: Low Detection Probability (LPI) radars are characterized by their low peak power, large time-bandwidth product, frequency hopping, and complex modulation. They represent one of the most effective anti-jamming technologies in modern electromagnetic countermeasure (ECM) systems. The low detection probability characteristics of waveforms and the complexity of intra-pulse modulation pose significant challenges to signal detection and recognition in modern electronic support (ES) systems [3] [5] [6] [7] [8]. Therefore, developing effective methods for LPI radar signal detection and modulation recognition has become two urgent requirements for modern ES systems.
The traditional RF processing chain of LPI radar signals in an ES system is shown in Figure 1 (a). Initially, the signal detection step receives and detects radar pulses in a stream manner. Then, in the parameter estimation step, the envelope of the detected signal is used to estimate the arrival time (TOA) and pulse width (PW). For each detected pulse, time-frequency analysis (TFA) is applied [7] [9] [10] [11] to perform modulation recognition, which allows the identification of the modulation type [12]. In the RF processing chain, signal detection and modulation recognition are two main tasks, and the parameter estimation step can be completed based on the detection and recognition results.
Radar Signal Detection (SD) is a task that evaluates whether there are radar pulses in a processing frame. For LPI signal detection, the fundamental and constant goal is to minimize the detectable signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [13]. Early attempts were based on statistical feature methods, including energy detection [14] time-frequency domain detection [9] [15] [16] cyclic shifting detection [17] and visibilogram-based methods [18]. These methods have poor performance at low SNR and their high computational complexity limits their deployment in online applications.
The task of automatic modulation recognition (AMR) for low-intercept probability signals is another research hotspot. The AMR task involves the classification of pulse-in-pulse modulation types for each intercepted pulse. Currently, the widely used AMR framework typically combines two steps of feature extraction and classification in a joint harvester. Early methods relied on handcrafted features and simple classifiers [28], [29], and performed poorly in increasingly complex waveforms and noisy electromagnetic environments.
Therefore, the DL-based AMR method has attracted attention due to its efficient feature learning capability. Various CNN-based deep networks have been designed to use TFI as input for performing AMR [3] to [40], and promising results have been reported. The high computational complexity generated by TFI limits the real-time processing capabilities of these methods, and subtle features in the original waveform may be obscured after time-frequency analysis. Therefore, the JMRPE proposed in [41] utilizes time features as input, while [42] introduces the use of autocorrelation features.Despite the sequence-based methods having resolved computational complexity issues related to TFI, directly processing raw sequences still negatively impacts recognition performance due to time series signals often being more complex. Therefore, it is necessary to explore network architectures with stronger feature extraction capabilities suitable for radar signal sequences to enhance recognition performance.
The aforementioned research primarily views the SD and AMR tasks as two cascaded tasks: firstly, detecting pulses, then sending AMR processing. However, the SD task is challenging because energy distributions are used for LPI purposes in both time and frequency domains. The energy distribution patterns are modulated-specific, and this information is not utilized in traditional detection methods for SD. Therefore, a reasonable and appealing idea would be to process the modulation characteristics jointly of these two tasks to assist in detecting LPI radar signals, which would also simplify the traditional processing chain. To this end, this paper proposes a multitask learning (MTL) framework that can effectively perform SD and AMR on LPI radar signals simultaneously.More specifically, the proposed Joint Detection and Modulation Recognition Network (JDMR-Net) accepts I/Q format pulse sequences as input and simultaneously determines the time position and modulation type of each detected pulse. In this way, the processing flow can be simplified from the front-end processing of SD and AMR in Figure 1(a) to the single block shown in Figure 1(b). Additionally, a dual-scale LSTM and Cross Attention (CA) structure has been designed for the JDMR network, which allows for the extraction of deep features between the pulse itself and the sequence of pulses. Comprehensive experiments combining simulation and real-world signals have validated the effectiveness and efficiency of this method, particularly under non-ideal conditions.
The main contributions of the proposed JDMR-Net can be summarized as follows.
1) A MTL framework is proposed to jointly achieve SD and AMR tasks for LPI signals, providing accurate time-domain localization and corresponding modulation types for a series of pulses.
2) By designing local feature extraction and global similarity mining modules, the JDMR network can extract intrinsic features within individual pulses and between pulse sequences, achieving higher detection probability and recognition accuracy under lower signal-to-noise ratios.
3) The simplified architecture and high degree of parallelism of this model make it an excellent candidate solution for online application deployment.
